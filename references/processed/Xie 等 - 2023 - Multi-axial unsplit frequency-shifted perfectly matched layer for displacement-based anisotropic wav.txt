Multi-axial unsplit frequency-shifted perfectly matched layer for 
displacement-based anisotropic wave simulation in inﬁ nite domain

Xie Zhinan1, 2†, Zheng Yonglu1, 2‡, Paul Cristini3§ and Zhang Xubin1, 2*

              1. Key Laboratory of Earthquake Engineering and Engineering Vibration, Institute of Engineering Mechanics, 

                  China Earthquake Administration, Harbin 150080, China

Abstract:  Multi-axial  perfectly  matched  layer  (M-PML),  known  to  have  lost  the  perfect-matching  property  owing  to 
multi-axial coordinate stretching, has been numerically validated to be long-time stable and it is thus used extensively in 
linear  anisotropic  wave  simulation  and  in  isotropic  cases  where  the  PML  becomes  unstable.  We  are  concerned  with  the 
construction of the M-PML for anisotropic wave simulation based on a second order wave equation implemented with the 
displacement-based numerical method. We address the beneﬁ t of the incorrect chain rule, which is implicitly adopted in the 
previous derivation of the M-PML. We show that using the frequency-shifted stretching function improves the absorbing 
eﬃ  ciency  of  the  M-PML  for  near-grazing  incident  waves.  Then,  through  multi-axial  complex-coordinate  stretching  the 
second  order  anisotropic  wave  equation  in  a  weak  form,  we  derive  a  time-domain  multi-axial  unsplit  frequency-shifted 
PML (M-UFSPML) using the frequency-shifted stretching function and the incorrect chain rule. A new approach is provided 
to  reduce  the  number  of  memory  variables  needed  for  computing  convolution  terms  in  the  M-UFSPML.  The  obtained 
M-UFSPML is well suited for implementation with a ﬁ nite element or the spectral element method. By providing several 
typical examples, we numerically verify the accuracy and long-time stability of the implementation of our M-UFSPML by 
utilizing the Legendre spectral element method.

Keywords: computational seismology; seismic anisotropy; wave propagation; elastodynamics

In  the  M-PML,  aside  from  damping  in  the  direction 
that  is  customary  for  interfaces  situated  between  the 
M-PML  and  the  truncated  domain,  additional  nonzero 
damping in tangential directions is introduced. Although 
tangential  damping  is  usually  set  to  be  much  smaller 
than that of normal damping, an important drawback is 
that the M-PML is not perfectly matched (Dmitriev and 
Lisitsa, 2011). Therefore, the M-PML can be interpreted 
as a compromise between the PML and absorbing layers, 
with  the  same  level  of  tangential  and  normal  damping 
(Cerjan  et  al.,  1985;  Sochacki  et  al.,  1987;  Semblat  et 
al.,  1987;  Halpern  et  al.,  2011).  To  achieve  the  same 
level of accuracy, M-PML is often more computationally 
eﬃ  cient than is the case with standard absorbing layers, 
but  less  eﬃ  cient  than  PML.  Gao  and  Huang  (2017) 
developed  a  numerical  algorithm  to  determine  the 
optimal level of tangential damping. 

First-order  M-PML  cannot  be  directly  used  in 
the  second-order  wave  equation  simulation  with 
displacement-based numerical simulation methods, such 
as the ﬁ nite element (FE) method (e.g., Bao et al., 1998), 
the spectral element (SE) method (e.g., Komatitsch and 
Tromp,  1999),  or  some  ﬁ nite  diﬀ erence  methods  (e.g., 
Moczo  et  al.,  2002).   Festa  and  Vilotte  (2005)  showed 
how  to  couple  the  ﬁ rst-order  split  PML,  which  was 
implemented  by  using  the  staggered  ﬁ nite  diﬀ erence 
method.  The  ﬁ rst-order  split  PML  was  implanted  into 
the second-order wave equation, implemented with the 
FE/SE  method  and  the  Newmark-beta  time-stepping 
scheme, by using the equivalence between the Newmark 
time-stepping scheme and the midpoint diﬀ erence rule. 
Following Festa and Vilotte′s observation, Martin et al. 
(2008) extended the ﬁ rst-order convolutional frequency-
shifted  PML  to  the  multi-axial  case  and  then  coupled 
it  with  the  second-order  wave  equation  simulation, 
employing the SE method and the Newmark-beta time-
stepping  scheme.  Such  a  coupling  remains  an  open 
problem  in  the  sense  that  other  time-stepping  schemes 
are used (Liu and Zhang, 2019).

stretching  for  the  second-order  wave  equation.  Li  and 
Bou  (2010)  developed  a  second-order  convolutional 
frequency-shifted M-PML in the frequency domain. In 
principle, Li and Bou’s formulation is not ﬁ t for layered 
heterogenous  inﬁ nite-domain  truncation  because  they 
treated the gradient of all convolutional terms in stress as 
additional body forces. Ping et al. (2014, 2016) extended 
the second-order split PML proposed by Komatitsch and 
Tromp (2003) for the multi-axial case. The formulation 
of   Ping  et  al.  (2014,  2016)  inherited  the  shortcomings 
of  the  PML  of  Komatitsch  and  Tromp  (2003),  arising 
from the introduced hybrid second-order and third-order 
ordinary  diﬀ erential  equations.  The  number  of  split 
displacement variables is at least ﬁ ve times that of the 
original variables (Ma and Liu, 2006). Moreover, Ping 

et al. (2014, 2016) obtained an inconsistent result that, 
unlike the obtained M-PML with a damping parameter 
deﬁ ned in the high-order polynomial proﬁ le, the M-PML 
with a damping parameter deﬁ ned in the commonly used 
second-order  polynomial  proﬁ le  loses  its  stability  in  a 
long-term simulation. Fathi et al. (2015) showed without 
theoretical  justiﬁ cation  that  although  the  chain  rule 
prevalently used in the M-PML derivation is incorrect, 
the resulting M-PML performed better than the M-PML 
derived by utilizing the corrected chain rule.

In this paper, we develop a second-order multi-axial 
unsplit  frequency-shifted  PML  (M-UFSPML). We  ﬁ rst 
show  the  importance  of  introducing  the  non-rigorous 
chain  rule  to  stabilize  M-UFSPML.  Additionally,  the 
introduction  of  frequency-shifted  coordinate  stretching 
also  can  improve  the  absorbing  eﬃ  ciency  of  the 
M-PML.  We  then  derive  the  M-UFSPML  by  multi-
axial  complex-coordinate  stretching  the  second-order 
wave  equation  in  its  weak  form—rather  than  using 
the  strong  wave  equation—to  avoid  possible  improper 
matching between the obtained PML wave equation and 
boundary or interface conditions. Improper matching is 
an important mechanism because it leads to an unstable 
PML (Duru and Kreiss, 2014). We introduce a new way 
to  reduce  the  number  of  memory  variables  needed  for 
dealing  with  the  computation  of  convolution  terms. 
The obtained M-UFSPML is in a weak form and thus is 
ready for implementation using the FE/SE method. We 
numerically verify the accuracy and long-time stability 
of  the  M-UFSPML  through  theoretical  and  practical 
applications  in  wave  simulation  with  the  high-order 
Legendre spectral element method and the explicit time-
stepping scheme.

3.2  Derivation of the second-order M-UFSPML

The PML is derived classically through the complex 
coordinate  stretching  of  the  wave  equation  written  in 
strong form. Recently, the weak-form approach for the 
PML derivation has been introduced. In the weak-form 
approach,  the  PML  is  derived  through  the  complex 
coordinate stretching of the wave equation written in the 
weak form (Bindel and Govindjee, 2005; Matuszyk and 
Demkowicz, 2013; Xie and Zhang, 2017). In the classical 
way,  the  derivation  of  the  wave  equation  in  the  PML 
and that of the boundary and/or interface conditions are 
independent, and the two may be improperly matched, 
which  can  lead  to  numerical  instability  and  reduced 
numerical accuracy, as shown by Duru and Kreiss (2014). 
However,  in  taking  the  weak-form  approach,  such  a 
mismatch can be naturally avoided because the free and 
interface  conditions  are  simultaneously  stretched  in  a 
consistent way. In the following sections, we derive the 
second-order M-UFSPM using the weak-form approach. 
We  start  from  the  second-order  wave  equation  in  the 
weak form speciﬁ ed in the inﬁ nite domain 

incident  waves,  which  is  important  for  low-frequency 
seismic  wave  simulation  adopted  in  seismic  hazard 
analysis.  Moreover,  we  show  the  importance  of  the 
complex frequency-shifted stretching function also can 
improve  the  absorbing  eﬃ  ciency  of  M-PML  for  near-
grazing incident waves. Based on the above inspection, 
our  M-UFSPML  is  derived  through  the  multi-axial 
complex  stretching  of  the  second-order  anisotropic 
wave equation written in its weak form. The formulation 
readily works with standard displacement-based FE and 
SE  wave  simulation,  with  only  minor  modiﬁ cation  of 
the  code  in  the  computational  domain.  It  is  simple  to 
implement and allows for a natural application of traction-
free boundary or interface conditions. The accuracy and 
long-time stability of the formulation was demonstrated 
using high-order SE discretization with a second-order 
explicit  Newmark  time  integrator,  which  showed  the 
accuracy  of  the  method  and  its  ability  to  solve  wave 
propagation  in  anisotropic  media  having  orthorhombic 
symmetry. Although the interface of the M-PML is not 
perfectly matched, it remains more eﬃ  cient at absorbing 
both body waves and interface waves than do low-order 
absorbing boundary conditions, such as the widely used 
Stacey  ABC  in  the  ﬁ eld  of  engineering.  One  topic  of 
interest  for  future  study  would  be  to  extend  this  work
