A multiaxial perfectly matched layer (M-PML) for the long-time
simulation of elastic wave propagation in the second-order equations

Contents

In order to conquer the spurious reﬂections from the truncated edges and maintain the stability in the long-time
simulation of elastic wave propagation, several perfectly matched layer (PML) methods have been proposed in the
ﬁrst-order (e.g., velocity–stress equations) and the second-order (e.g., energy equation with displacement un-
known only) formulations. The multiaxial perfectly matched layer (M-PML) holds the excellent stability for the
long-time simulation of wave propagation, even though it is not perfectly matched in the discretized M-PML equa-
tion system. This absorbing boundary approach can offer an alternative way to solve the problem of the late-time
instability, especially for anisotropic media, which is also suffered by the convolutional perfectly matched layer (C-
PML) that is supposed to be competent to handle most stable problems. The M-PML termination implementation
in the ﬁrst-order formulations is well proposed. The common drawback of the implementation of the ﬁrst-order
M-PML formulations is that it necessitates fundamental reconstruction of the existing codes of the second-order
spectral element method (SEM) or ﬁnite element method (FEM). Therefore, we propose a nonconvolutional
second-order M-PML absorbing boundary condition approach for the wave propagation simulation in elastic
media that has not yet been developed before. Two-dimensional numerical simulation validations demonstrate
that the proposed second-order M-PML has good performances: 1) superior efﬁciency and stability of absorbing
the spurious elastic waveﬁelds, both the surface waves and body waves, reﬂected on the boundaries; 2) superior
stability in the long-time simulation even in the isotropic medium with a high Poisson's ratio; 3) superior efﬁciency
and stability in the long-time simulation for anisotropic media. This method hence makes the SEM and FEM in the
second-order wave equation formulation more efﬁcient and stable for the long-time simulation.

developed in wave propagation modeling in recent decades, which are

necessary techniques to suppress spurious reﬂections from the truncat-
ed edges of a ﬁnite-sized discrete earth model. The classical ABCs,
‘sponge zones’ (Cerjan et al., 1985; Sochacki et al., 1987) and paraxial

the elastic properties of the earth model as well, obviously affect the ab-

The perfectly matched layer (PML), introduced by Bérenger (1994),
was ﬁrstly used in Maxwell equations. In order to make it possible to in-
corporate mathematical formulations for desired absorbency, the ﬁeld
variables of the PML are split into nonphysical components. Because of
the advantages of independences on the angle and the frequency of in-
cident waves, researchers promote the PML method in different wave
propagation modeling problems, as electromagnetic wave propagation
(e.g., Bérenger, 1996, 1998; Collino and Monk, 1998; Harari et al.,
2000; Teixeira and Chew, 1998a, b), acoustical wave propagation (e.g.,
Hesthaven, 1998; Hu, 1996; Nataf, 2006), as well as elastic wave propa-
gation in elastic media (e.g., Basu and Chopra, 2004; Chew and Liu,
1996; Collino and Tsogka, 2001; Festa and Nielsen, 2003; Hastings
et al., 1996), in viscoelastic media (Bécache et al., 2004; Martin and
Komatitsch, 2009), and in poroelastic media (Martin et al., 2008; Zeng
et al., 2001).

Although the numerical validations of this method are successful,
the PML still faces many challenges, e.g., poor absorbency for strong re-
ﬂections at grazing incidences, and instability for the long-time simula-
tion (Festa et al., 2005; Komatitsch and Martin, 2007; Matzen, 2011).
These problems have been intensively investigated in recent years
(Halpern et al., 2011). For example, due to the divergence of the PML so-
lution under small perturbations (Abarbanel and Gottlieb, 1997), it was
initially thought that noncausality of the constitutive parameters makes
dynamical instability of the classical PML (Kuzuoglu and Mittra, 1996).
The convolutional perfectly matched layer (C-PML), an alternative caus-
al frequency dependent PML, was hence proposed and particularly in-
vestigated (Abarbanel and Gottlieb, 1998; Appelö and Kreiss, 2005;
Basu and Chopra, 2004; Gedney, 1996; Komatitsch and Martin, 2007;
Li and Matar, 2010; Matzen, 2011; Zhao and Cangellaris, 1996;
Ziolkowski, 1997). The absorbency of the reﬂected waves from incident
outgoing waves on artiﬁcial boundaries at grazing angle is improved
(Bérenger, 2002; Festa and Vilotte, 2005; Komatitsch and Martin,
2007; Martin and Komatitsch, 2009; Martin et al., 2008; Roden and
Gedney, 2000). However, the C-PML cannot solve all the instable prob-
lems (Komatitsch and Martin, 2007). It is believed to suffer from the
late-time instability, especially for anisotropic media (Matzen, 2011).
Meanwhile, the C-PML loses its absorptive capacity in an elastic medi-
um at low frequencies (Meza-Fajardo and Papageogiou, 2008). The
multiaxial PML (M-PML) method, which is efﬁcient and stable without
dependence on the frequency and the direction of wave propagation,
was hence developed (Meza-Fajardo and Papageogiou, 2008, 2010,
2012). The M-PML method is based upon a more general coordinate
stretching version of the classical split-ﬁeld PML, in which the waves
are absorbed in all directions with different damping factors. Zeng et al.
(2011) validated the excellent stability of the M-PML method in the
media with high Poisson's ratios. Due to effective absorption of the
spurious reﬂections, even though it is not perfectly matched in the
discretized M-PML formulation system (Meza-Fajardo and Papageogiou,
2012), the M-PML is implemented for anisotropic elastic media with
good performance (Dmitriev and Lisitsa, 2011, 2012). The M-PML meth-
od has been veriﬁed to be more stable and efﬁcient than the classical PML
and the C-PML in the long-time simulation of wave propagation in iso-
tropic and anisotropic media in the ﬁrst-order equation formulation
(Meza-Fajardo and Papageogiou, 2008). Meza-Fajardo and Papageogiou
(2012) further pointed out that increasing the layer thickness has very
important impact on (i.e., is more effective in) reducing reﬂections.
Through their numerical examples, it illustrated that the overall reﬂec-
tions of the M-PML are the same or less than that obtained when the
classical PML and the C-PML are used.

For the second-order wave equations, however, the ﬁrst-order M-
PML cannot be applied straightforwardly. The common drawback of im-
plementation of these formulations is that it necessitates fundamental
reconstruction of the existing codes of the ﬁnite element method
(FEM) or spectral element method (SEM) in the second-order equations.
In addition, the reconstructed codes of the FEM or SEM may in common
consume more computing resources, due to system matrices with extra

non-zero entries (and broken symmetries) implying growth in storage,
and the number of ﬂops to be performed at each time step, since, apart
from displacements, stresses additionally become the unknowns. Fur-
thermore, most of the existing FEM software for solid mechanical prob-
lems solves the wave equations for the displacements only. Therefore,
it is an urgent need that the PML formulations are established for the
second-order elastic wave equations. Komatitsch and Tromp (2003)
ﬁrstly introduced a classical PML for the second-order wave equations
in elastic media. Their PML is highly absorbent for the surface waves in
elastic media, but unstable in the long-time simulation. Li and Matar
(2010) proposed a non-split C-PML for the second-order mixed wave
equations in elastic media. In reality, their scheme formulations are
also needed in reconstructing the existing second-order FEM or SEM
codes. Matzen (2011) explicitly investigated non-split C-PML formula-
tions for the second-order wave equations in elastic media. He believed
that the second-order C-PML would suffer from the late-time instabilities
in anisotropic media. To our best knowledge, the PML approaches to con-
quer the late-time instabilities for wave simulation in the second-order
equations, especially for anisotropic media, are rather limited. Whereas
the M-PML has offered an applicable way to solve the problem in the
ﬁrst-order equations (Meza-Fajardo and Papageogiou, 2008, 2012;
Zeng et al., 2011), however, the applications of the M-PML in the context
of the second-order wave equations are not explicitly studied. Thus, the
promotion of the M-PML into the second-order equations has still to be
suggested to meet the urgent need.

Due to the split components in the second-order wave equation are
not linearly independent but coupling, there is no appropriate treat-
ment of the corner regions (see Fig. 2) in the classical second-order
PML formulations at present. Straightforwardly to add the damping var-

no longer applicable. In order to handle the damping in the corner re-
gions for the second-order classical PML, we have to reconstruct the
PML formulations for damping the waveﬁelds in different directions at
the same time. The damping variables of the coupling terms in the equa-
tions face complicate mathematical procedure, while in the proposed
second-order M-PML formulations (Eq. (28)), such difﬁculty is over-
come in the uniform formulations.

The treatment of the corner regions for the proposed second-order
M-PML is similar to that of the ﬁrst-order M-PML (Meza-Fajardo and
Papageogiou, 2008). Moreover, in corner regions, the formulations of
Eq. (28) are replaced by seven independent equations (see Appendix).

5. Numerical validation

To illustrate the efﬁciency and the stability of the second-order M-
PML formulations, we consider four numerical experiments of the elas-
tic wave propagation with P-SV polarization in two-dimensional
models with free surface. The wave equations in the regular region
and the M-PML region are solved with the spectral element method
(SEM). The accuracy of the proposed scheme is demonstrated by the
comparison of the numerical and the analytical solutions in an isotropic
half-space model. The results for the classical second-order PML are also
presented for comparison. In the ﬁrst two numerical experiments, we
investigate the stability of the second-order M-PML for the isotropic
half-space models. A high Poisson's ratio, which may yield unstable
issue in the implementation of some ABCs, especially for Rayleigh
wave modeling (Zeng et al., 2011), is considered. In the other two ex-
periments, we simulate wave propagation in the anisotropic media,
which may cause severely unstable issues.

For Case 1 of the isotropic half-space models, the size of the model is
192 m × 192 m. The source locates at (62.4 m, 0 m) on the free surface.
The Ricker wavelet function is selected for the source time function with
the central frequency of 50 Hz, and the time shift of 0.04 s. The PML ab-
sorbing boundaries are set in vertical and bottom edges (Fig. 2) with the
span of 38.4 m. The model is discretized in 40 × 40 spectral elements
with polynomial degree of N = 4. The physic parameters of the model
are listed in Table 1.

For Case 2 of the isotropic half-space models, the size of the model is
55 m × 40 m. The source locates at (18.0 m, 0 m) on the free surface.
The Ricker wavelet function is also selected with the central frequency
and the time shift of 40 Hz and 0.04 s. The PML absorbing boundaries
are set in vertical and bottom edges (Fig. 2) with the span of 8 m. The
model is discretized in 55 × 40 spectral elements. The polynomial de-
gree N = 4 is chosen in each element. The physic parameters of the
model are listed in Table 1.

For Case 3 of the anisotropic half-space models, the size of the model
is 616 m × 448 m. The Ricker wavelet source locates at (201.6 m, 0 m)
on the free surface, with the central frequency of 22.5 Hz, and the time
shift of 0.044 s. The PML absorbing boundaries are set 67.2 m in width
around the three edges (see Fig. 2). The model consists of 55 × 40 spec-
tral elements with polynomial degree of N = 4. The physic parameters
of the model are listed in Table 2.

6. Conclusions

A nonconvolutional perfectly matched layer, referred to as the mul-
tiaxial perfectly matched layer (M-PML) for the second-order elastic
wave equations, has been proposed and numerically validated. The pri-
mary of this work is to show that an efﬁcient M-PML implementation is
indeed possible when e.g. the existing displacement-based (second-
order) SEM or FEM codes need to tackle the limited region elastic
wave simulation problems. It is veriﬁed that the absorbing boundary
represented in the second-order wave equations can be well construct-
ed by the M-PML formulations. The present scheme is stable and efﬁ-
cient, and allows us to simulate the elastic waveﬁelds, especially in
anisotropic media for long time using the spatial second-order wave
equations in the SEM, FEM and FDM.

Through the comparison with the second-order classical PML pro-
posed by Komatitsch and Tromp (2003), better stability is achieved by
the second-order M-PML formulations in the several numerical

M-PML scheme leads to its adaptability in other second-order formula-

scheme to three-dimensional problems is straightforward and will be
studied in the future.
